# Notes Protocol

## Purpose and Source Authority

Reconstruct the teachable course for revision, including material a student may be learning for the first time. Keep Notes broader than a past-paper trend report.

Apply the user's source roles before drafting. Build the Notes from the complete extracted coverage authority in one pass. When lecture slides are primary and text notes are reference material, cover every substantive slide knowledge point available in the fragment index; use reference notes for emphasis, concise phrasing, and useful organisation.

Past papers and recap questions calibrate emphasis and explanation depth. They do not replace course coverage.

## Public Notes Boundary

Represent course knowledge through definitions, structures, mechanisms, processes, taught methods, equations, evidence, data interpretation, comparisons, applications, and explanatory examples.

Use assessment material internally for emphasis. Keep assessment strategy, command-word coaching, answer-planning routes, thesis or paragraph templates, model-answer scaffolds, reproduced question banks, revision schedules, time-allocation plans, and exam-day advice in a separately requested Practice or Essay artifact.

Generate only the Notes file for Notes tasks. Do not create a companion Practice or Essay artifact automatically.

## Coverage and Slide Triage

Build connected knowledge units from definitions, mechanisms, methods, comparisons, calculations, data interpretation, evidence, applications, and explanatory examples. Use learning outcomes, agendas, section dividers, recaps, and summaries for structure. Merge repetition and continuation slides into the nearest complete explanation.

Use automated `use`, `merge_with_previous`, and `exclude` triage as the default drafting input. Inspect a complete rendered slide or page only when extraction is missing or garbled, an exclusion is materially uncertain, or an essential visual cannot be interpreted from the extracted record. Do not manually confirm every slide or maintain a fragment-by-fragment decision ledger by default.

## Visual Value Gate

Use a diagram, graph, micrograph, process schematic, compact comparison table, or equation only when it materially improves explanation of a spatial, temporal, structural, quantitative, or causal relationship. There is no image quota.

Prefer a suitable original lecture-slide image, crop, or complete slide page when it already teaches the relationship clearly. Create a new diagram only when the supplied material has no adequate visual.

Use conservative embedding sizes and readable source material. Do not calculate or record per-image effective font sizes by default. If a retained visual is visibly unreadable at the intended width, enlarge, crop, simplify, split, or omit it during initial composition.

Keep a slide title complete or remove it completely; never leave a partially cropped slide title. Do not print source filenames, slide locators, or source legends unless requested. Supply meaningful alternative text for retained teaching visuals.

## Writing, Equations, and Tables

Choose paragraphs, learning-point lists, or a combination according to the material. Coherent longer paragraphs are appropriate when they explain a connected concept more clearly. Define specialist terms at first use, show causal chains explicitly, and place methods and worked examples inside the knowledge unit they support.

Let knowledge density, conceptual difficulty, and explanation needs determine length and detail. Do not expand or compress to meet a uniform page, paragraph, or bullet target.

Set important formulae as centred display equations. Render fractions, roots, Greek letters, operators, subscripts, superscripts, and chemical charges as mathematical notation. Define symbols and units immediately below and carry units through calculations.

Use tables only when exact comparison or mapping is clearer than prose. Apply content-aware widths, natural wrapping, restrained borders, suitable padding, and alignment based on content. Prefer simpler tables during initial generation rather than repairing overcrowded tables afterward.

## Document Design

Use A4, Arial 11 pt body text, 2 cm margins, 1.5 line spacing, a centred 20 pt main title, left-aligned 15.5 pt first-level headings, 13 pt second-level headings, and 11.5 pt third-level headings. Use 10 pt table text and 9 pt captions by default, justified black body text, restrained hierarchy, and consistent visual placement.

Keep the document in natural continuous flow. An intentional page break may introduce an actual Lecture boundary; knowledge units within the same lecture flow continuously.

Use the requested filename and language. Otherwise use English and a clear course-based filename.

## One-Pass Completion

Generate the document once with the rules above, then run one bounded sanity check:

- the file exists and opens;
- the requested title and major knowledge units are present;
- the real format is correctly declared;
- no unresolved placeholders, extraction failures, or generation errors remain.

Do not render the complete DOCX to page images or inspect every page by default. Use targeted rendering only when the user explicitly requests visual QA or the renderer reports a concrete layout error. Inspect the affected page or element and apply at most one targeted correction; report any remaining limitation instead of repeating a render-repair loop.
